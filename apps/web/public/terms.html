The terms of using TrustIQ

Last updated 1 September 2026. This describes the closed beta.

TrustIQ is run from Sharjah, in the United Arab Emirates, by the person who built it. Using the app or the site means these terms apply to you. They are written to be read, so they are short and they say what is not true as well as what is.

These have not been reviewed by a lawyer. They describe the product accurately, which is the part that can be got right without one. The clauses on liability and on which law applies are the ones that need a UAE firm to look at them, and that review is coming. Until it has happened, treat those two sections as a statement of intent rather than as settled drafting.

What TrustIQ is

TrustIQ is a record. You write down what you and somebody else agreed, you file the documents that show what happened, and if it goes wrong you can ask for a resolution to be proposed.

TrustIQ is not a party to your agreement. The agreement is between you and the other person. TrustIQ writes it down, timestamps it, fingerprints what you file against it, and refuses to let either of you change it afterwards. It does not promise that the other person will do what they said, and it cannot make them.

Whether what you wrote down amounts to a contract a court would enforce depends on what you agreed and on the law, not on this app. TrustIQ makes the record clear. It does not make the agreement valid.

TrustIQ does not hold your money

Payment is between the two of you, directly, by whatever means you choose. TrustIQ never receives it, never holds it, and never moves it. There is no wallet, no balance and no account to top up.

This is deliberate. Holding somebody else's money in the UAE is a licensed activity, and TrustIQ is not licensed for it. Escrow is planned for a later version through a partner who is licensed. It is not in this one, and nothing in the app should be read as suggesting your money is protected or held anywhere.

A resolution is a proposal

When a dispute is opened, TrustIQ can ask a model to read both accounts against the evidence you both filed and propose how the disputed amount should be divided. It usually answers within a minute.

That proposal only ends the dispute if both of you accept it. One refusal from either side is enough, and the case then goes to a person to review.

What it is not:

  • Not arbitration. No award is being made and nothing is binding on you unless you accept it.
  • Not legal advice. It is a reading of the documents in front of it, not advice about your rights.
  • Not always available. The system refuses to propose anything when the evidence is thin, when it is not confident enough, or when the amount is above a ceiling. Those cases go to a person instead. More than half do.

Accepting a proposal is you agreeing with the other party about how to settle. Carrying it out — paying, refunding, delivering — is between the two of you, for the same reason as above.

Who can use it

You need to be at least 18 and able to enter an agreement on your own behalf. If you are using TrustIQ for a business, you need to be allowed to commit that business.

TrustIQ is built for the United Arab Emirates. You can use it from elsewhere, but nothing here is adapted to another country's rules.

Your account, and being verified

One account per person, with an email address you control. Keep the password to yourself; what happens under your account is treated as done by you.

Asking to be verified means telling us your legal name and which document you are prepared to show. A person reviews it. Nothing is automated, no document is uploaded, and no document number is stored. Verification says a human checked something; it is not a guarantee about anybody's honesty, and you should not treat a verified badge as one.

What you agree not to do

  • Use TrustIQ for anything unlawful, or to agree to anything unlawful.
  • Pretend to be somebody else, or set up a contract in a name that is not yours to use.
  • File a document you know to be false, altered, or not yours to share.
  • Upload anything abusive, threatening, or designed to harass the person on the other side.
  • Try to reach data that is not yours, or to get around the checks that keep it that way.
  • Automate signups, flood the service, or resell access to it.

If somebody does any of that to you, there is a report action on any contract. It reaches a person at TrustIQ and the other party is not told you used it. You can also refuse future contracts from somebody, which stops either of you starting anything new with the other. It does not touch a contract you are already in: you may need that record precisely because it went wrong.

What you file stays filed

Evidence cannot be edited or withdrawn once it is submitted, and neither can the terms of a contract both of you accepted. This is the point of the product rather than a limitation: a record either side could quietly revise afterwards would be worth nothing to either of you.

Think before you file. What you upload is visible to the other party to that contract, and to a reviewer if the case reaches one.

You can close your account, from the app or from the deletion page. What that removes and what it cannot is set out in the privacy notice — in short, your account and your personal details go, and a contract cannot be erased out from under the other person who is party to it.

When an account is suspended or closed

An account can be suspended if there is good reason to think these terms are being broken, or that somebody is being harmed. A written reason is required every time and is kept on a record that cannot be edited afterwards. Suspension stops you signing in; it does not delete anything, and it does not release you from an agreement you have already made with somebody.

You are not currently sent a notice when it happens. You find out because signing in stops working. That is a gap rather than a decision, and it will be closed. If it happens to you, write to the address at the bottom of this page and you will be told the reason that was recorded.

What TrustIQ does not promise

This is a closed beta run by one person. It is offered as it is:

  • No promise that it will be available, or fast, or working at any given moment.
  • No promise that a resolution will be proposed, or that a proposal will be one you like.
  • No promise about the other party's honesty, solvency, or intention to pay.
  • No security certification. Nothing here has been independently audited, and the site does not claim otherwise.

What is promised is narrower and worth more: what you filed is what everybody sees later, the terms you both accepted cannot be changed afterwards, and every run of the resolution model is recorded whether it succeeded or not.

If something goes wrong with TrustIQ itself

This section needs a lawyer's eye and has not had one. It says what is intended. UAE consumer protection law may not allow parts of it, and where the two conflict, the law wins and this paragraph does not.

TrustIQ is not responsible for the agreement you made, for whether the other person keeps it, or for money that passed between you. What TrustIQ is responsible for is the service it runs: keeping the record intact, not showing your data to people who should not see it, and not claiming things about the product that are untrue.

Nothing here limits liability for fraud, for death or personal injury caused by negligence, or for anything else the law does not allow to be limited.
