What TrustIQ knows about you

Last updated 28 August 2026. This describes the closed beta.

TrustIQ records what two people agreed, so that neither has to remember it the same way. It never holds your money. This page says what it stores to do that, who else sees it, and what it does not have.

What is stored

Your account

  • Your name, which the other party to a contract sees.
  • Your email address, used to sign in and to reach you.
  • Your password, held by our authentication provider and never visible to us.
  • Which language you chose, so mail is written in it.

Your identity check

A contract only becomes binding between two verified identities. During the closed beta somebody at TrustIQ checks your documents and records that they did, along with a note saying what they looked at. Your profile is marked as a manual check and never as a UAE Pass one, so nobody reading it later mistakes the two.

Your Emirates ID number is not stored. It is visible to whoever checks it and goes no further. It identifies you across every system in the country, and keeping a copy would make this database worth attacking for reasons that have nothing to do with TrustIQ.

Your contracts

  • What you wrote: the description, the terms, the amount, any stages.
  • Every move either party makes, with who made it and when.
  • Documents you file as evidence, and a SHA-256 fingerprint of each, which we calculate from the bytes we stored rather than accept from you.
  • The text inside a document, where the file is one we can read, so a dispute can be judged against what it says.
  • What each of you wrote about a dispute, in full, shown to the other.

Somebody you invite

If you address a contract to an email with no TrustIQ account, we store that address so the invitation can be claimed by that person and nobody else. We do not write to them. You send them the code yourself.

What is not stored

  • Payment details, card numbers or bank accounts. TrustIQ handles no money.
  • Your Emirates ID number, address, nationality or date of birth.
  • Your location.
  • Your contacts, photos or files, beyond the documents you deliberately file.
  • Any advertising or analytics identifier. The app contains no analytics library, no advertising library and no tracker of any kind.

Who else sees it

The other party

Your name, whether you are verified, and everything on a contract you share: its terms, its history, the documents either of you filed and what each of you said about a dispute. Nobody outside a contract can see any of it. That is enforced by the database rather than by the app.

A reviewer at TrustIQ

Only when a dispute is escalated to a person, and only from that moment. A reviewer sees the claims, the evidence and the history. They are deliberately not shown either party's name.

Three companies we depend on

  • Supabase hosts the database and the files.
  • Brevo delivers email. It receives your name and address, and nothing about your contracts.
  • Anthropic analyses a dispute when one is opened. It receives the agreed terms, the amount, the dates, what each of you wrote about the dispute, and the text of the documents filed. We send it no name, no email address and no account identifier. What it sees of you is only what you or the other party typed: if either of you writes a name into a claim, that goes as written.

Your data is stored in Germany, in Supabase's eu-central-1 region, not in the UAE. If where your data physically sits matters to you, it should weigh on whether you use the beta.

How long it is kept

Contracts, their history, the documents filed against them and the record of identity checks are append-only. They cannot be edited or removed, by either party or by TrustIQ. That is the point of the product: a record one side could quietly delete would be worth nothing to the other.

You can close your account from inside the app, under the menu, and it happens immediately. Your name, your address, your phone number and your identity verification are erased, and you cannot sign in again.

If nothing at all points at your account it is deleted outright. If you have been party to a contract, that contract stays, along with the documents filed against it and what each side said about any dispute, attached to a record that no longer names you. Not because we want to keep it, but because it belongs to the other person as much as to you: a record one side could destroy is worth nothing to the other.

The full detail is on its own page, including what to do if you cannot get into the app.

Your rights

Ask us for a copy of what we hold about you, ask us to correct it, or ask us to stop using it. We will answer within thirty days. Where a request conflicts with a record the other party depends on, we will say so rather than quietly refuse.

Children

TrustIQ is for adults entering agreements. It is not for anyone under 18 and we do not knowingly hold data about anyone who is.

When this changes

The date at the top moves and the change is described in the app. This page is versioned alongside the code that implements it, so the two cannot drift apart without somebody noticing.
